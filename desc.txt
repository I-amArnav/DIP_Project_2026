algo1
Classical Line Completion for Normal (Full-Thickness) Line Art:
    1. Binarization
    2. Internal skeletonization only for reliable endpoint detection
    3. Distance transform on normal lines for accurate stroke thickness estimation
    4. Gap bridging with thickness-matched lines and smooth caps on normal image

algo2
Direction-aware classical line-art completion, v2 (no neural network).

What is new vs v1 (found via the failure overlays: bridges were drawn from
skeleton endpoints that hook toward one corner of a thick stroke's cut face,
giving tilted / shifted bridges, and gaps allowed to grow too much with width)

  1. Radius-aware spur pruning: a skeleton branch is removed if it is shorter
     than ~1.5x the stroke radius at the junction (kills the forks that
     concave cut ends create on thick strokes).
  2. Robust tangent: a PCA line fit to skeleton points BEHIND the tip zone
     (skipping the first ~radius px, where the skeleton hooks).
  3. Tip refinement: the fitted centre-line is marched outward until it leaves
     the stroke, giving the true centre of the cut face (not the skeleton end).
  4. Pair cost now also penalises lateral offset (both strokes must line up
     within about one stroke width) and stroke-width mismatch.
  5. Bridges are Bezier curves from just inside one cut face to just inside
     the other, thickness = mean stroke width, so they merge seamlessly.
  6. "T" bridges (endpoint -> side of another stroke) march along the tangent
     through background until they hit foreground, over a shorter range.

Works on full-thickness and 1-px skeletonized inputs.

evaluate.py
Line-completion evaluation v2 (tolerance-aware, gap-event aware).

Changes vs evaluate_tolerant.py
  * "Missing" and "added" pixels are now defined PER tolerance tau, so the
    corrupted input scores exactly 0 gap recall at every tau (no more
    artefacts at tau > gap_def).
  * Signed component-count difference (positive = still fragmented,
    negative = over-merged) next to the absolute error.
  * Gap-EVENT metrics: each removed stroke piece is one "gap", each newly drawn
    piece is one "bridge".
        Gap close rate     = gaps restored (>= close_frac of pixels within tau)
        Bridgeable close   = same, only for gaps with a stroke on BOTH sides
                             (the ceiling for any endpoint-based method)
        Bridge precision   = bridges that lie on the GT (>= close_frac within tau)
        Event F1           = harmonic mean of close rate and bridge precision
    Pooled totals are printed too, so you can quote e.g. "812/1040 gaps closed,
    131/980 bridges wrong".

generate_inputs
Structure-aware synthetic gap generator for line-art completion.

Differences from the old random-circle eraser:
  * Gaps are cut ALONG a stroke (a capsule that follows the skeleton path), so
    each gap fully severs exactly one stroke and does not eat neighbours.
  * Gap length is controlled (in px and relative to local thickness), so it
    matches what the completion algorithms are meant to bridge.
  * Gaps are never placed near junctions/corners or near stroke ends, and each
    side of a gap keeps a minimum stub length (so a tangent can be estimated and
    the ground truth is unambiguous).
  * Gap count scales with skeleton LENGTH, not with stroke pixel count, so
    thick-line images are not over-damaged.
  * Gaps never overlap each other and never wipe out a whole short stroke.
  * Deterministic: per-image seed = hash(filename) + --seed.
  * Optionally writes a binarized clean GT (--gt-dir) and a gap mask (--mask-dir)
    so evaluation compares like with like and can be restricted to gap regions.

make_overlay
Failure overlays + diagnosis for line completion.

For each selected image, saves a side-by-side panel:
    [ corrupted input | overlay | algorithm result ]
Overlay colours (all measured on skeletons, tolerance tau):
    GREEN  correct bridge   (newly drawn, within tau of the ground truth)
    RED    false bridge     (newly drawn, farther than tau from the ground truth)
    BLUE   missed gap       (ground-truth stroke removed by the corruption, not restored)
    dark gray  existing strokes in the input,  light gray  ground-truth strokes

It also prints a diagnosis over ALL images: how many gaps were closed / missed,
how many of the missed ones were actually bridgeable (strokes on both sides),
how long correct vs false bridges are, and whether false bridges are
"misaligned" (close to the GT but off) or "spurious" (nowhere near any GT stroke),
followed by tuning hints for algo.py.
